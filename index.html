Research Papers Notes CV

Department of Electrical & Computer Engineering
University of Massachusetts Amherst

Abstract. I study quantum error correction and fault-tolerant quantum architectures under physical hardware constraints. My current work asks how memory lifetimes, gate errors, probabilistic entanglement generation, and classical communication shape the design of quantum networks. I focus on sparse hybrid repeaters and asynchronous QEC, building on earlier work on geometry-dependent electric-field noise in trapped-ion devices. I am an M.S. student in ECE and a graduate research assistant with Filip Rozpędek at UMass Amherst.

1.Current work

QEC / networks

Hybrid quantum repeaters

How sparsely can better memories and local error correction be deployed while retaining useful network performance? Joint studies of hardware, placement, routing, and synchronization.

QEC / architecture

Asynchronous quantum error correction

Ongoing work on QEC and decoding when entanglement arrives at different times: heralded failures, memory age, syndrome scheduling, and the cost of waiting.

trapped ions

Geometry-controlled correlated electric-field noise in enclosed ion traps

Geometry-dependent correlated electric-field noise, enclosure effects, Green-function kernels, and billiard return spectra in trapped-ion devices.

QEC / graph states

Repair identifiability and product-measurement complexity

Which graph-state fault mechanisms can be distinguished from product-Pauli data, and how measurement context, retained statistics, Pauli weight, and noise constrain repair diagnosis.

QEC / statistics

Predictive rank as a memory witness for QEC syndrome records

Circuit separator bounds, finite-sample rank tests, and conditional model checking on public Google surface-code records.

QEC / geometry

Covariance geometry of linearized quantum erasure correction

Grassmannian geometry of the exact Knill–Laflamme locus, covariance-controlled normal structure, leakage Hessians, and code rigidity.
