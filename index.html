Electrical & Computer Engineering · University of Massachusetts Amherst

Research. I study quantum error correction and fault-tolerant architectures under physical hardware and network constraints. My central question is how codes, logical operations, and schedules should be designed together when entanglement arrives probabilistically and quantum memories keep aging.

I am an M.S. student and graduate research assistant in ECE at UMass Amherst. I work on hybrid quantum repeaters with Filip Rozpędek and Stav Haldar and am a member of Don Towsley's ACQuIRE lab. My research combines mathematical analysis and simulation, building on an undergraduate background in engineering, mathematics, and statistics and earlier work on trapped-ion electric-field noise.

1.Research directions

Asynchronous quantum error correction

How can a code and its measurement schedule protect quantum information when links and checks become available at different times? Current work examines heralding information, accumulated memory noise, and the conditions for safe execution.

Hybrid quantum repeater architectures

How much high-quality hardware does a quantum network need? With Filip Rozpędek and Stav Haldar, I study how memory quality, local error correction, placement, and routing jointly affect fidelity and rate.

Device geometry and correlated noise

How electrode geometry shapes electric-field noise and spatial correlations in ion traps, using Green-function models and numerical geometry diagnostics.
