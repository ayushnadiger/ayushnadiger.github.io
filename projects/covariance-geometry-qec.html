Covariance geometry of linearized quantum erasure correction

started May 2025 · manuscript August 2026 · quantum error correction / differential geometry

Abstract. Exact erasure correction imposes Knill–Laflamme scalar-compression constraints on a code subspace. I study their linearization on the Grassmannian of codes. The central result is that the Gram operator of the vertical differential is exactly the covariance operator of the erased marginal, tensored with the identity on traceless logical observables. This determines the local normal geometry of exact correction.

1.Main idea

A K-dimensional code is a point of Gr(K,N). For a fixed erased subsystem A, exact Knill–Laflamme correction is the zero set of a natural bundle-valued section. At a full-rank exact code, the vertical differential of that section has covariance-controlled normal form: up to orthogonal normal coordinates, the map is twice the square root of the covariance tensor of the erased state.

This gives transversality of the exact-code locus, an explicit normal Hessian for a quadratic leakage functional, and a reduced normal condition number. For qubit erasure, that condition number is the reciprocal of the qubit-versus-rest concurrence.

2.Rigidity

For several allowed erasures, the linearized constraints stack into a deformation complex. Exact symbolic calculations for the [[4,2,2]] and [[5,1,3]] codes show that the kernel of the stacked Jacobian is exactly the local-unitary orbit: both codes are infinitesimally rigid modulo local physical unitaries for the stated erasure constraints.

3.Origin

This line began with a differential geometry presentation in May 2025 on two-qubit state space, the Segre embedding CP¹ × CP¹ → CP³, and concurrence. The current QEC problem keeps the same geometric question—how a distinguished quantum locus sits inside an ambient projective/Grassmannian space—but replaces the separable variety by the exact-code locus.

4.Reproducibility

The analytic results are proved in the manuscript. A public verification suite checks the covariance-normal identity in random bases, the leakage identity, exact Knill–Laflamme conditions, and the advertised stacked-Jacobian calculations.
