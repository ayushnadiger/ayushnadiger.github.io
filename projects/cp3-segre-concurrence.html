CP³, the Segre embedding, and concurrence

May 2025 · differential geometry presentation · geometry / quantum information

Abstract. A pure two-qubit state is a point of complex projective three-space. Product states form the Segre quadric, and the familiar determinant condition for separability becomes a geometric equation. These notes began as a differential geometry presentation in May 2025 and collect the projective-geometric picture relating separability, Fubini–Study geometry, and concurrence.

1.Setup

Write a pure state as coefficients of a 2×2 matrix. Projectivizing removes the physically irrelevant overall complex scale. The separable locus is then exactly the rank-one locus, which is the image of CP¹ × CP¹ under the Segre embedding into CP³.

2.Concurrence

For a normalized pure state, concurrence is twice the absolute determinant of the coefficient matrix. Algebraically it vanishes on the Segre quadric. With the standard Fubini–Study normalization, it is a monotone function of the distance to the separable locus. This geometric viewpoint later became the starting point for a different question: what does the normal geometry of an exact quantum error-correcting-code locus look like?

3.From entanglement geometry to QEC

The current continuation replaces the Segre variety by the exact Knill–Laflamme zero locus inside a Grassmannian of code subspaces. Linearizing the erasure-correction constraints gives a normal operator controlled exactly by the covariance tensor of the erased marginal. For an erased qubit, concurrence reappears as the reduced normal condition number.
