Research

Quantum error correction, network architectures, and physical noise.

1.Asynchronous quantum error correction

Quantum networks make the timing of error correction part of the design problem. Entanglement generation is probabilistic, memories accumulate noise while waiting, and heralds and measurement records become available at different times. I am investigating how a code and its schedule can use this information to control uncertainty during execution while preserving protection against physical errors.

The work uses small stabilizer and CSS-code models and Pauli-frame simulations to examine measurement timing, decoding, and logical commitment. The broader goal is to identify code structures and fault-tolerant operations suited to probabilistic interconnects. A central evaluation question is whether an asynchronous protocol improves the fidelity–latency tradeoff at matched physical resources and logical-error targets.

2.Hybrid quantum repeater architectures

With Filip Rozpędek and Stav Haldar, I study how sparsely deployed nodes with better memories and local error correction can support useful quantum-network performance. The design couples memory coherence and gate quality to node placement, routing, and synchronization.

Current work connects analytical spacing estimates to chain simulations and develops extensions to two-dimensional networks and multiple paths. Fidelity, secret-key rate, completion time, and hardware requirements provide complementary measures of the design tradeoffs.

3.Trapped-ion noise and geometry

My honors thesis and subsequent preprint study how electrode geometry changes electric-field noise and spatial correlations for a fixed surface-noise spectrum. Green-function kernels and numerical geometry diagnostics connect device enclosure to coupling strength and anisotropy.

4.Related mathematical work

Additional manuscripts study what measurement records reveal about faults and memory, and the local geometry of exact error correction.

  • Repair identifiability in graph states — distinguishing fault mechanisms that require different physical repairs.
  • Predictive rank of QEC syndrome records — statistical witnesses of memory with circuit contributions made explicit.
  • Covariance geometry of erasure correction — local constraints, leakage, and code rigidity.
