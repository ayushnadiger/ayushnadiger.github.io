Research

From physical noise to reliable quantum networks.

1.Heterogeneous quantum network architectures

My current work with Filip Rozpędek asks how sparsely expensive memories and local error correction can be deployed while preserving useful network performance. I study hybrid repeater networks in one and two dimensions, keeping memory coherence, gate errors, link-generation probabilities, node placement, routing, and synchronization explicit. I compare fidelity, secret-key rate, completion time, and hardware requirements.

2.Asynchronous quantum error correction

Entanglement between network nodes arrives probabilistically, so qubits in the same code block can have different ages and noise histories. I am studying how heralding information, memory age, and measurement timing should enter QEC protocols and decoders. Starting with repetition codes and small CSS codes, this work examines when more general codes, including quantum LDPC codes, justify their connectivity and syndrome-extraction costs in a network. This is an ongoing research direction.

3.Trapped-ion noise and geometry

My honors thesis and subsequent preprint study how electrode geometry changes electric-field noise and spatial correlations for a fixed surface-noise spectrum. Green-function kernels and numerical geometry diagnostics connect device enclosure to coupling strength and anisotropy.

4.Related mathematical work

Additional manuscripts study what measurement records reveal about faults and memory, and the local geometry of exact error correction.

  • Repair identifiability in graph states — distinguishing fault mechanisms that require different physical repairs.
  • Predictive rank of QEC syndrome records — statistical witnesses of memory with circuit contributions made explicit.
  • Covariance geometry of erasure correction — local constraints, leakage, and code rigidity.
